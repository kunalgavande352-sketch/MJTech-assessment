Circadian rhythm and everyday life

Your body runs on an internal clock of roughly 24 hours. Light, routine, and sleep timing help keep it in step with your day. When the clock and the schedule disagree, sleepiness and trouble concentrating can follow.

Sleep timing

Regular sleep and wake times give the clock a steadier pattern to follow.

Light exposure

Daylight earlier in the day and dimmer evenings are the strongest cues for most people.

Daily routine

Meals, movement, and social time can act as secondary timing cues.

Metabolic health without the hype

Blood sugar, cholesterol, triglycerides, blood pressure, and waist size help clinicians understand how the body handles energy. Reference ranges exist for good reasons, but they are guides, not verdicts.

People vary, results fluctuate, and a single number only means something in context: age, family history, medications, and other measurements.

What a marker can show

  • Where a measurement sits against common reference ranges.
  • How a value changes over time.
  • Whether more testing may be worth discussing.

What it cannot show

  • A diagnosis on its own.
  • Your personal risk without clinical context.
  • Whether a supplement is right for you.

Healthy longevity: what research can tell us

Longevity headlines often blend very different kinds of evidence. Knowing which kind you are reading helps you decide how much weight to give it.

Observational associations

Groups are compared over time. Useful for spotting patterns, but other differences between groups can explain results.

Intervention evidence

Trials assign an intervention and compare outcomes. Stronger for cause, though often shorter than a human lifespan.

Unsupported promises

Claims to reverse aging or guarantee extra years usually rest on animal studies or testimonials.

Common questions

Short answers about the site, its tools, its sources, and what it cannot do.

Who is MJTech for?

Readers in the United States who want general, sourced information on sleep, metabolic health, hydration, and longevity — not personalized medical care.

Are the tools validated medical calculators?

No. They are planning and reflection aids. They are not risk scores, diagnoses, or substitutes for clinical instruments.

Where does the information come from?

Public agency pages, established professional guidance, and, when used, peer-reviewed papers with the study type labeled.

Do you store the health information I enter?

No. Answers stay in your browser for that session and are not sent to a server.

Do you have doctors or a medical review board?

No. We do not claim clinicians on staff, certifications, or institutional endorsements.

Can I use the site if I have a medical condition?

You can read general information, but follow your care team. Do not change medicines, fluids, or exercise based only on a webpage.
